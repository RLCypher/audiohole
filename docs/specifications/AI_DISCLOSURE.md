# AI Disclosure

### Yes, we used AI. A lot, actually.

AudioHole was developed with extensive assistance from artificial intelligence. We're not going to pretend otherwise, bury it in the fine print, or act like using modern tools is something to be embarrassed about.

AI played a significant role throughout development, particularly in technical research, investigating unfamiliar concepts, filling gaps in specialized knowledge, exploring potential engineering solutions, assisting with code development, and building the avatar animation pipeline for AudioHole's personality system.

That last one deserves special mention. The avatar engine is an entirely unnecessary, spectacularly silly little subsystem that exists almost exclusively because the developer has ADHD and apparently cannot be trusted to leave well enough alone. Does an audio routing application need animated characters with distinct personalities, moods, and reactions? Absolutely not. Did we build an entire animation pipeline for them anyway? Of course we did.

This should not be confused with the **DFA system**, which is an absolutely critical component of AudioHole's operational architecture. Its importance cannot be overstated. No further explanation will be provided.

In short, AI helped us figure out how to do a lot of things, including some things we didn't initially know how to do, and several things we probably didn't need to do in the first place.

**But there's an important distinction: AudioHole is AI-assisted, not AI-directed.**

The project's vision, requirements, priorities, architecture, and design decisions are human-driven. AI didn't decide what AudioHole should be, what problems it should solve, or what compromises were acceptable. Those decisions were made by an actual human, occasionally after arguing with a machine about them.

AI-generated code, research, and recommendations were reviewed, questioned, revised, and tested. Suggestions weren't accepted simply because a language model confidently insisted they were correct. Especially when they weren't.

And when something breaks, we're not going to blame the chatbot.

We believe AI is an incredibly useful tool for independent development, particularly when tackling problems that would otherwise demand a much larger team or expertise across numerous disciplines. Used responsibly, it can expand what a single developer is capable of accomplishing without surrendering creative control or engineering judgment.

**The ideas are human. The direction is human. The responsibility is human.**

The tools just helped us make the holes.

And if you think we missed something, got something wrong, or know a better way to do it, you're more than welcome to roll up your sleeves and fix it. Do it the old-fashioned way, or vibe code the fuck out of it. We don't particularly care how you get there, so long as it works, it's tested, and you can explain what the hell it does.

---

*No artificial intelligences were harmed in the making of AudioHole. Several were extensively questioned.*

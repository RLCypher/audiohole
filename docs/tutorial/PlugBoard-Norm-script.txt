INTEGRATION NOTES
- This is a script for the existing fictional tutorial sandbox, not a command to modify real Windows audio routing.
- Preserve the existing welcome, earlier device/profile lessons, and Settings walkthrough. The tutorial's previously scanned BoomSlab 9001 is the default playback device at the start of this insert.
- The practice application roster includes Spooty and Felony Jaywalking LXVII. All practice applications initially appear in the System socket. Other apps may be present; do not require specific unlisted ones.
- MixBoard Music and MixBoard Game are available as previously scanned pretend playback endpoints. The tutorial may accept any name for the newly created Music and Game sockets; advance by socket identity and endpoint, not literal socket label.
- All assignments, endpoints, and mixing behavior in this walkthrough are simulated. No real applications, devices, Windows mixer preferences, or persistent user boards are changed.
- Every beat has an expression, a cue, and authored dialogue. Use fallback expression: idle. Only advance after the required GUI interaction has completed and been verified. At choice beats, the selected button determines which variation of the NEXT scripted dialogue is displayed; never display a standalone choice reaction, require a separate Continue button, or insert another dialogue step.
- The existing personality response catalog is not a substitute for this tutorial. Suppress unrelated random event chatter while guided tutorial dialogue is playing.
- The System socket remains present and is not renamed or deleted. Do not require opening the settings drawer unless that is necessary to access socket creation in the implemented UI.
- For all cues, identify the actual UI control rather than relying on an assumed button location; highlight the relevant tab, socket controls, endpoint dropdown, or app row as appropriate.
- Treat successful simulated assignment separately from actual routing: never say real Windows playback was moved.

PLUG BOARD — A LITTLE APPLICATION ORGANIZATION
======================================================================

PB001 [talking]
Cue: Begin this insert after the preceding tutorial section. Highlight the Plug Board tab; require the user to open it.
NORM: We've connected devices. Now let's tell individual apps where to send their audio. Open Plug Board; I've got a small organizational problem for you.

PB002 [thinking]
Cue: Plug Board tab opened; highlight the socket columns.
NORM: These columns are sockets. Each points to an audio output, and you plug apps into whichever one you like. There's no limit to how many apps a socket holds.
UI: Show two choice buttons: "So they're categories?" / "As many as I want?". The chosen button advances directly to PB003, selecting its corresponding dialogue variant; do not display a separate reaction or Continue control.

PB003 — CHOICE-DEPENDENT NEXT LINE
Cue: Show the fake-app entries in System; highlight System and its BoomSlab 9001 endpoint.
IF PB002 CHOICE "So they're categories?" [talking]
NORM: Exactly. Categories with an output device attached. I've lent you a few of my totally legit apps to try it out; they're all in System, currently using the BoomSlab 9001.
IF PB002 CHOICE "As many as I want?" [smug]
NORM: As many as you please. Whether you can organize that mess is another question. I've put my totally legit practice apps in System, pointing at our BoomSlab 9001.
UI: Show two choice buttons: "Totally legit, huh?" / "Why System?". The chosen button advances directly to PB004, selecting its corresponding dialogue variant.

PB004 — CHOICE-DEPENDENT NEXT LINE [working]
Cue: Highlight the socket-creation control; require creation of one new socket after the line is displayed.
IF PB003 CHOICE "Totally legit, huh?" [suspicious]
NORM: They passed every inspection I performed in my imagination. Anyway, make a new socket for music; let's give those suspiciously legal tunes their own destination.
IF PB003 CHOICE "Why System?" [talking]
NORM: System catches new apps until you assign them somewhere else. Let's create a new socket for music, so it doesn't all have to go through System.

PB005 [talking]
Cue: New socket successfully created; highlight its rename action or editable name; require the user to rename it to any chosen name for music.
NORM: Give that socket a music-related name. Whatever makes sense to you; we're not grading the poetry.

PB006 [thinking]
Cue: Music socket renamed; highlight its playback endpoint dropdown; require selecting MixBoard Music.
NORM: Now set its output to MixBoard Music. That's the pretend mixer channel we'll use for music.

PB007 [happy]
Cue: Music socket assigned to MixBoard Music; require creation of another socket.
NORM: Good. Let's make a second socket for games, so the explosions don't have to share a channel with the playlist.

PB008 [working]
Cue: Second socket created; highlight rename action; require any game-related name distinct from the music socket.
NORM: Name this one for games. Again, any sensible label will do.

PB009 [talking]
Cue: Game socket renamed; highlight endpoint dropdown; require selecting MixBoard Game.
NORM: Give the game socket MixBoard Game as its destination. One channel for games, one for music. Even I can keep track of that.

PB010 [smug]
Cue: Game socket assigned to MixBoard Game; highlight Felony Jaywalking LXVII in System and the game socket; require moving that application to the game socket by drag-and-drop or its context menu.
NORM: Find Felony Jaywalking LXVII in System and move it to your game socket. I still don't understand the hype. People act like it's the sixty-seventh coming.

PB011 [deadpan]
Cue: Felony Jaywalking LXVII is assigned to the game socket; highlight Spooty in System and the music socket; require moving Spooty to the music socket.
NORM: There. Civilization survives. Now move Spooty into your music socket so it gets its own mixer channel.

PB012 [happy]
Cue: Spooty is assigned to the music socket; highlight the two new sockets and their device dropdowns.
NORM: And there it is. Game and music now feed separate channels on our pretend mixing board, so you can control them independently from the external device.
UI: Show two choice buttons: "So I can balance them separately?" / "And other apps work the same way?". The choice advances directly to PB013 and selects its corresponding dialogue variant; no standalone reaction or Continue control.

PB013 — CHOICE-DEPENDENT NEXT LINE
Cue: Tutorial's Plug Board task completed. Remove required-action highlights; allow optional sandbox exploration. Highlight Settings as the next destination; wait for the user to open it.
IF PB012 CHOICE "So I can balance them separately?" [talking]
NORM: Exactly. Separate physical volume controls for games and music. Feel free to experiment, and meet me in Settings when you're done. One last thing to show you.
IF PB012 CHOICE "And other apps work the same way?" [smug]
NORM: Yep. Move an app to a socket and it follows that socket's destination. Play around if you like; I'll be in Settings with one last thing to show you.

PB014 [idle]
Cue: User opens Settings after PB013. Resume the existing Settings tutorial sequence at its first Settings beat; do not replay its prior sections.
NORM: Ah, there you are. Last stop: Settings.
